# Executive Summary

On September 29, 2024, a significant chemical fire occurred at the Bio-Lab, Inc. facility in Conyers, Georgia. The incident was triggered when a faulty sprinkler system released water onto trichloroisocyanuric acid (TCCA), a highly water-reactive chemical, resulting in a violent chemical reaction, fire, and the release of toxic chlorine gas. While there were no reported injuries or fatalities, the plume affected air quality and led to the evacuation or shelter-in-place orders for tens of thousands of residents. The incident highlighted critical lapses in chemical storage safety, emergency preparedness, and regulatory oversight. This document provides a comprehensive analysis of the incident, its causes, consequences, regulatory outcomes, and recommendations for improved laboratory safety.

# Detailed Research Document: Bio-Lab, Inc. Safety Incident (Conyers, Georgia, 2024)

## 1. Incident Overview

- **Date:** September 29, 2024
- **Location:** Bio-Lab, Inc., Conyers, Georgia, USA
- **Type of Incident:** Chemical reaction and fire at a chemical/biological manufacturing facility
- **Key Chemical Involved:** Trichloroisocyanuric acid (TCCA)
- **Immediate Trigger:** Malfunction of sprinkler system releasing water onto TCCA

## 2. What Happened

- At approximately noon, a defective sprinkler head activated, spraying water onto pallets of TCCA.
- TCCA, used in pool chemicals, reacts violently with water to release toxic chlorine gas and heat.
- The reaction led to an intense fire and the formation of a large, visible chemical plume.
- Emergency responders arrived quickly, ordering an evacuation of up to 17,000 residents and a shelter-in-place for as many as 90,000 people in the affected area ([CSB Investigation Update PDF](https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/6/investigation_update_bio-lab_final_nov2024_(005).pdf), [CSB Update](https://www.csb.gov/us-chemical-safety-board-releases-investigation-update-into-september-2024-massive-fire-and-toxic-plume-at-bio-lab-facility-in-georgia/)).

## 3. Place Details

- **Bio-Lab, Inc.** manufactures pool and spa treatment chemicals.
- The facility stored large quantities of TCCA and other hazardous chemicals.
- The plant has a history of safety violations and had previously experienced multiple chemical fires (notably in 2004 and 2016) ([11Alive Report](https://www.11alive.com/article/news/local/biolab-chemical-fire-conyers-georgia-2004-similar-incident/85-d71cc6d6-78cf-4283-9510-03c93b2ee71c)).

## 4. Incident Causes

### Immediate Cause

- **Water exposure to TCCA:** The accidental activation of a sprinkler system led to direct water exposure to TCCA.
- **Chemical reaction:** TCCA (trichloroisocyanuric acid) reacts with water to release chlorine gas and heat, causing fire and toxic smoke.

### Underlying Lapses

- Inadequate assessment of compatibility between fire suppression systems and stored chemicals.
- Deficient maintenance and inspection of sprinkler and alarm systems.
- Insufficient risk analysis and hazard reviews for water-reactive materials.
- Poor historical safety record and lack of corrective action after previous incidents.

#### Technical Note
- *TCCA* stands for **trichloroisocyanuric acid**, a chemical widely used in pool and spa disinfection products. When exposed to water, it decomposes, producing toxic chlorine gas and nitrogen trichloride ([NJ.gov Fact Sheet](https://nj.gov/health/eoh/rtkweb/documents/fs/qr/1892qr.pdf)).

## 5. Consequences

- **Casualties:** No injuries or fatalities were reported among employees or the public ([Atlanta Journal-Constitution](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1049928106641176/posts/1207679504199368/); [CSB Investigation Update PDF](https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/6/investigation_update_bio-lab_final_nov2024_(005).pdf)).
- **Evacuations:** Up to 17,000 residents were evacuated; as many as 90,000 people were advised to shelter-in-place ([CSB](https://www.csb.gov/us-chemical-safety-board-releases-investigation-update-into-september-2024-massive-fire-and-toxic-plume-at-bio-lab-facility-in-georgia/)).
- **Health Impact:** Georgia Poison Control received over 330 calls reporting symptoms (respiratory distress, eye irritation) attributed to the chlorine plume ([AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/atlanta-news/georgia-chemical-plant-fire-rockdale-county-ends-shelter-in-place-order/NLTZ6RLMIZCERF76B7FHUQFRYE/)).
- **Environmental Impact:** The EPA and Georgia EPD monitored air quality, detecting elevated chlorine and hydrochloric acid levels but reported no long-term contamination ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/ga/conyers-ga-biolab-fire)).
- **Property Damage:** The fire caused extensive damage to the facility, with insurance claims and lawsuits exceeding $20 million ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2025/12/09/850314.htm)).
- **Community Impact:** Displacement, anxiety, economic disruption, and ongoing health monitoring for affected residents.

## 6. Root Cause Analysis

- **Primary Root Cause:** Lack of compatibility assessment for fire suppression systems with stored chemicals (water-based sprinklers + reactive TCCA).
- **Systemic Issues:** Repeated safety culture failures, insufficient regulatory compliance, and ineffective hazard communication.
- **Historical Pattern:** The 2024 incident is the fourth major fire at this facility since 2004, pointing to a longstanding pattern of inadequate risk management ([John Foy & Associates](https://www.johnfoy.com/faqs/how-many-fires-has-biolab-had/)).

## 7. Laboratory Safety Protocols and Prevention Measures

### Existing Protocols

- Segregated chemical storage.
- Fire suppression systems (primarily water-based sprinklers).
- Emergency response and evacuation plans.

### Protocol Lapses

- Failure to use compatible fire suppression for water-reactive chemicals.
- Lax maintenance and infrequent review of safety-critical infrastructure.
- Inadequate emergency notification to local authorities.

### Prevention Measures

- **Compatibility Engineering:** Use non-water-based fire suppression (e.g., dry chemical or gas systems) for water-reactive chemicals.
- **Routine Inspections:** Mandatory, documented maintenance of all suppression and alarm systems.
- **Chemical Segregation:** Strict physical separation of water-reactive materials from moisture sources.
- **Comprehensive Hazard Assessments:** Regular, third-party risk reviews.
- **Community Drills:** Joint exercises with local emergency services and public health agencies.

## 8. Lessons Learned

- Matching suppression systems to chemical hazards is essential.
- Regular, independent risk assessments must be enforced, especially after repeat incidents.
- Transparent communication and community engagement during emergencies are critical for public trust.
- Similar incidents, such as the 2013 West Fertilizer Company explosion in Texas and Bio-Lab’s own 2004 fire, demonstrate the dangers of inadequate chemical hazard controls and the need for industry-wide vigilance ([11Alive](https://www.11alive.com/article/news/local/biolab-chemical-fire-conyers-georgia-2004-similar-incident/85-d71cc6d6-78cf-4283-9510-03c93b2ee71c)).

## 9. Recommendations

1. **Upgrade Fire Suppression**: Replace water-based systems with dry chemical or inert gas suppression in areas storing water-reactive chemicals.
2. **Mandatory Hazard Audits**: Require annual, third-party risk assessments for all chemical storage and suppression systems.
3. **Enhanced Chemical Segregation**: Institute physical and procedural barriers to prevent accidental mixing of incompatible substances.
4. **Improved Emergency Communication**: Establish rapid notification protocols for both internal stakeholders and the surrounding community.
5. **Community Preparedness**: Regularly involve local agencies and residents in drills and education initiatives.
6. **Regulatory Compliance and Oversight**: Enforce stricter penalties for repeated safety violations and require transparent public reporting of corrective actions.
7. **Post-Incident Monitoring**: Implement long-term health and environmental surveillance for impacted communities.

## 10. Regulatory and Policy Follow-up

- The U.S. Department of Labor (OSHA) cited Bio-Lab for improper storage of hazardous chemicals ([OSHA News Release](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/osha/osha20250407-0)).
- The Georgia Environmental Protection Division (GA EPD) documented multiple violations and required corrective action plans ([Atlanta Business Chronicle](https://www.bizjournals.com/atlanta/news/2024/10/03/biolab-conyers-multiple-violations-epd-records.html)).
- Insurance lawsuits and class actions against Bio-Lab and its parent company sought over $20 million in damages ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2025/12/09/850314.htm), [Sokolove Law](https://www.sokolovelaw.com/personal-injury/biolab-fire/)).
- As of late 2025, Bio-Lab ceased manufacturing at the Conyers facility, though hazardous chemicals remain on-site ([HealthBeat](https://www.healthbeat.org/atlanta/2025/06/03/biolab-fire-chemical-lawsuit-manufacturing/)).
- Over 29,000 compensation claims were filed by affected residents ([Scalawag Magazine](https://scalawagmagazine.org/2025/11/biolab-fires-corporate-greed-and-environmental-injustice-in-georgia/)).

## 11. Comparative Context: Similar Incidents

- **Bio-Lab Fires (2004, 2016):** Similar water-chemical reactions, large evacuations, and toxic plumes occurred at the same facility, indicating persistent risk management failures ([11Alive](https://www.11alive.com/article/news/local/biolab-chemical-fire-conyers-georgia-2004-similar-incident/85-d71cc6d6-78cf-4283-9510-03c93b2ee71c)).
- **West Fertilizer Company Explosion (Texas, 2013):** Catastrophic explosion due to improper storage of ammonium nitrate; resulted in 15 deaths and over 200 injuries. Both incidents underscore the critical importance of chemical compatibility and regulatory enforcement.
